The live trip

Rupees as you drive

A trip starts when the engine starts and ends when it is switched off, and standing at a signal or in a jam never ends it. If the engine restarts within five minutes, it is the same trip. While it runs, TapeDash keeps:

Cost so far

Whole rupees, with confidence pips beside the figure that say how far to trust it.

Per kilometre and per litre

Rupees a kilometre, the average km/L, and an instant km/L that shows a dash below about 5 km/h.

Idling

Idle time and idle cost, updated live, with the idle counter pulsing gently while you stand.

What is left

The fuel remaining in rupees and kilometres, when the car reports its level.

Where it went

Every rupee goes in exactly one bucket

Each moment's fuel cost lands in one bucket and one only, so the four always add up to the trip. The shapes differ as well as the colours, so nothing relies on colour alone.

Normal driving

Everything that is not one of the others. A solid champagne block.

Idling, which is avoidable

Speed under 2 km/h with the engine running. A hollow amber block, because money burned for nothing looks empty.

Hard acceleration

Spent during a hard-acceleration event. A solid red block with a notch.

High speed

Above your speed threshold, 100 km/h by default. A solid accent block.

The wording is deliberate. Idling is "avoidable". The others are "spent during hard acceleration" or at high speed, and nothing is ever called "wasted".

Honest numbers

Estimates, and they say so

Whole rupees, one decimal

Rupees are rounded to whole numbers and km/L to one decimal, never more. Parked detail views prefix an estimate with an approximation sign.

Confidence pips

One lit cell means uncalibrated, two means calibrating, three means calibrated. How a fill-up calibrates it.

No price, no rupees

Until you set a fuel price, the figures are litres, with one button that asks for the price. You type it in, and it is never fetched.

Unavailable means unavailable

A car that reports nothing TapeDash can turn into fuel gets a plain explanation, not an invented figure.

How it is held to account

Tested against the simulator's exact truth

The simulated car keeps exact fuel, distance and idle time. The tests compare the app's estimates with that, and the build fails if they drift:

  • Trip distance within 2% of the truth.
  • Fuel within 5% where the car reports fuel rate or air flow, and within 10% by speed-density before any calibration.
  • Idle time within 5% and idle cost within 7%.
  • The same route driven gently costs at least 15% less than driven hard, which is the premise of the coach.
  • The buckets sum to the trip total, and the Money Tape's rupee blocks match the buckets.

Said plainly

What has been seen

Seen

  • The tests above, on the simulator, for every petrol profile.
  • A real car with the air-flow method: the money screens ran on a real phone, and the idle cost of about 1.3 litres an hour matched what the car's air flow implied over two long drives.

Not yet

  • A comparison against a real tank. Until then, a real car's figures are the model's estimates and may be off by several percent.
  • The ethanol correction for E20 petrol. It is tested against the simulator and has not been checked on a pump.
  • The parked Money page's layout. In the simulator renders, up to 800 wide, the breakdown's legend can crowd the central readouts.
