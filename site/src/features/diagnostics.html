What your car is saying

Three panels on one screen

The adapter

Its name and version, the protocol in words, the module voltage, the achieved poll rate as a sparkline, and which fuel method is in use.

The PID matrix

One cell for each Mode 01 reading from 01 to 60, lit where your car answers. Tap a cell to name it. It shows exactly what your car is talking about, and so what TapeDash can use.

Fault codes

Stored codes (Mode 03) and pending codes (Mode 07), each with a plain-English line and a stored or pending chip.

Fault codes

Read them in words, not numbers

A code like P0420 is decoded into its family (powertrain, chassis, body or network) and looked up in a list bundled in the app. The list of 283 common generic codes was written from scratch for this project, in short plain sentences, and records its source in the repository. It is deliberately not exhaustive.

Manufacturer-specific codes mean different things on different cars. For those, and for any generic code that is not in the list, the app says which system the code belongs to and to look it up for your car. It never guesses a description.

Clearing

A long slide, and only when stopped

Clearing codes (Mode 04) needs a full slide to confirm, and the app will not do it unless the car is at a standstill. If the car refuses, you are told. Clearing also wipes the data a mechanic would want, so do not use it on a car you do not understand.

What has been seen

Seen

  • The whole path against a simulated car with two codes stored (P0420 and P0171): they are read, decoded and described, and clearing is refused while the car moves.
  • Every control at 640, 720 and 800 wide, and the flow through the real app.

  • The PID matrix does not yet pulse on each response, and its voltage is the module voltage the poller reads, not the adapter's own reading.
