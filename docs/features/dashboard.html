Features / On the dash

The dashboard

TapeDash is for people who know the best instrument clusters in the world and want that on their own dash. A tach arc, tapes, a road view and a telemetry grid, drawn with a glyph atlas so they do not jitter, on a phone that may be ten years old.

  • Simulator
  • Emulator
  • Real phone
  • Real car (one BLE adapter)

Drive

Five layouts, one swipe

A vertical swipe cycles through the layouts and the choice is remembered. They share one horizon and floor, so changing layout never moves the things you read by feel.

Look

Four liveries, and a mode for the sun and the dark

A livery swaps the accent colour, the numeral style and the texture, and never the layout or the motion. Daylight and Night apply on top of any livery by themselves, from the phone's light sensor, and can be overridden in Garage.

Instruments

Each part is an instrument, not a widget

Moving and parked

It gets out of the way, and comes back when you stop

The app is one strip of pages: Review, Money, Drive, Coach, History. While the car moves only Drive and Money can be reached, and the page dots lock. A stop of five seconds, or the ignition off, puts the dock in reach.

When the car starts moving, the moving layout is back within 300 ms.

Starting up, and losing the car

Old phones first

Smooth on a ten-year-old phone

A glyph atlas

Live numerals are drawn from a prebuilt atlas, with baked glow sprites and cached static layers, so nothing is laid out as text every frame.

Zero allocation per frame

Live data never rebuilds a widget. Painters repaint through listenables and batch their segments into one draw.

Three fidelity tiers

The app measures the phone and picks a tier, with a 60 fps target and a 30 fps fallback. The look has to hold up in the lowest tier.

Budgets

Under 20 MB per ABI with fonts included, about 150 MB of memory, 32-bit ARM kept, Android 7 as the floor.

Said plainly

What has and has not been seen

Seen

  • All five layouts, the liveries and both modes, drawn by the real app in tests and captures, and on an Android 7 emulator and a real phone.
  • A real car over several long drives with a Bluetooth Low Energy adapter, with the page dots locking while moving.
  • Frame timing on a flagship phone: about 120 fps with the full tier chosen automatically.

Not yet

  • The old, low-end 2 GB phone the app is designed for. Timings are from emulators and a flagship, which say little about it.
  • Other adapters. The one tried polls about 1.3 times a second, so the needle is smoothed with a playout buffer that shows the car about half a second late, on purpose, to avoid overshoot.
  • Every livery and mode in the golden-image matrix on every page.

TapeDash is a free, open-source, strictly offline Android app. Fuel and money figures are estimates. It is an aid, not a certified instrument: set it up while parked, and you remain responsible for driving safely.
