The windshield HUD

Stick the phone face up on the dash, lay a reflector film or a 3D-printed holder over it, and the speed floats in the windshield. The HUD is one component of the design, not the whole of it: windshield optics have their own rules.

As designed. The HUD is drawn mirrored, because it is read in a reflection.

Optics

What a windshield lets through

A windshield reflects only a small fraction of the phone's light, and the two glass surfaces of a laminated screen add a faint double image. Only bright, bold, simple shapes survive. So the HUD is not the dashboard made bigger. It has its own rules.

Pure black

No background, no frames, no etched lines. Nothing of the phone's own glass is lit, so only the numerals reach the windshield.

One colour

HUD Green (the most visible, and the default), Ion, Amber or White. You choose it; a critical alert still uses red.

Bold and large

Strokes of at least 5 lp, labels at least 6% of the screen height, numerals SemiBold or heavier. No halos, no trails, no thin lines.

No bright areas

Numerals may be filled, panels never, so a night drive gets no glare and no double-imaged blocks.

Layouts

Three, and a fourth when Maps is guiding

While Google Maps is guiding, the HUD takes over with its navigation layout and gives your own layout back when Maps stops. A double-tap on Drive or Money, or the dock button, switches the HUD on and off, and the last mode is remembered. The screen is kept on, set to maximum brightness in daylight, and put in immersive mode.

Setting it up

An alignment pattern, and every way up

However the phone sits, it should work. Garage has a flip left to right (on by default, because a windshield mirrors), a flip top to bottom, the height on the glass, and the size. Before you drive, a full-screen test pattern with a crosshair grid, a large F and an UP marker lets you confirm the orientation and position in the actual windshield. It is drawn with your current flips.

Brightness

Bright by day, quiet at night

In daylight the HUD goes to maximum brightness. At night it follows the room's light with a floor of 20%, so a dark road gets a dim, steady figure. The numerals roll on a single gentle spring and nothing overshoots.

Said plainly

Not yet seen on a windshield

Seen

  • Every layout and the alignment pattern, drawn by the real app, as designed and mirrored, at 640, 720 and 800 wide.
  • Tests that hold the HUD to one colour on pure black, strokes of at least 5 lp, and nothing under the size floor.
  • The HUD on an Android 7 emulator and on a real phone at a desk.

Not yet

  • A real windshield: its brightness in sun, the double image of laminated glass, and whether a reflector film or a printed holder helps.
  • Night driving with the HUD on.
  • The HUD's heat and battery over a long drive in the sun.
