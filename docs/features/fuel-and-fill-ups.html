Features / Where the money goes

Fuel, fill-ups and calibration

A cheap adapter cannot tell you the litres, so TapeDash works them out from what the car does report, shows which method it used, and lets your fill-ups correct it. The figures are estimates and say so.

  • Simulator, against ground truth
  • Emulator
  • Against a real fill-up: not yet

Where the litres come from

It uses the best thing your car reports

An ELM327 adapter reads what the engine computer will say. Most cars do not say how much fuel they burn, so TapeDash picks the best available method by itself and shows which one is in use on the Diagnostics page.

1. Best

Fuel rate (PID 5E)

The engine's own figure, used as it is. Never adjusted for ethanol.

2. Common

Air flow (MAF)

Litres an hour from the air the engine breathes, divided by the air-fuel ratio and the fuel's density.

3. Fallback

Speed-density

From manifold pressure, intake temperature and revs, with your engine's size. Many petrol cars with no MAF work this way.

None of the three? Then fuel and money show as unavailable, with a plain explanation. A diesel's air-fuel ratio varies, so the air-flow method is not valid for it: it uses the fuel rate if there is one, and otherwise says the money is low-confidence until a fill-up calibrates it.

Indian petrol

Ethanol changes the maths

Petrol at Indian pumps is increasingly E20. Ethanol changes both the air-fuel ratio and the density, so the same air means more litres:

E0, plain petrol

Air-fuel ratio 14.7, density 740 g/L.

E10

Ratio 14.1, density 745 g/L: about 3.6% more litres for the same air.

E20, the default for a new install

Ratio 13.5, density 750 g/L: about 7.5% more litres for the same air. Without this, every figure would read about 7% low until fill-ups corrected it.

Pit Stop

Log a fill-up, and the estimate learns your car

Pit Stop is a number pad, and it opens only when the car is stopped. Enter the litres and the amount paid, and say whether the tank was filled full. The price a litre is the amount divided by the litres, and it becomes the current price. You can also set the price directly without logging a fill-up.

Calibration

Two full tanks make it honest

Between two consecutive full-tank fill-ups, the app compares the litres you actually put in with the litres it estimated you burned. The ratio is a factor. It keeps a rolling average of the last three factors, held between 0.7 and 1.3, and applies it to every fuel estimate.

  • Uncalibrated: no factor yet. One pip lit.
  • Calibrating: one factor. Two pips.
  • Calibrated: two or more. Three pips.

On the simulator, after two simulated full-tank fill-ups the calibrated estimate comes out within 3% of the truth on every petrol profile, and the build fails if it does not.

The count of litres in the tank, for a car that sends no fuel level, is a separate Insight. It starts from the last full tank, so it needs this log.

Seen

  • The estimators against hand-computed values, and the whole chain against the simulator's exact fuel, with each petrol profile and with calibration.
  • The air-flow method on a real car, which was the fuel source on the first real drives.

TapeDash is a free, open-source, strictly offline Android app. Fuel and money figures are estimates. It is an aid, not a certified instrument: set it up while parked, and you remain responsible for driving safely.
