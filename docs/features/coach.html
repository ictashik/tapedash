Features / Learning from your drives

The driving coach

It does not nag. While you drive it shows one wordless cue. When the car is stopped or the trip is over, it points at the one or two things that cost you the most, in rupees, and never calls anything wasted.

  • Simulator
  • Emulator
  • Judged on real drives: not yet

The one rule

It never speaks while you drive

A coach that nags is a distraction, and a distraction is a danger. So while the car moves the coach is limited to one wordless cue, and detailed advice appears only when the car is stopped or the trip has ended. No pop-ups, no text entry, no small tap targets, no voice.

That is the live cue. It compares your instant km/L with your car's own average, so it needs no fuel price and nothing about anyone else's car.

What it notices

Five kinds of moment

The speed is smoothed over about a second before acceleration is worked out, because OBD speed is whole km/h and noisy. Every threshold is configurable, and Garage has Gentle, Normal and Sporty presets.

Hard acceleration

Smoothed acceleration above 2.5 m/s2 for at least a second, or throttle above 70% for two seconds above 10 km/h.

Hard braking

Smoothed deceleration beyond 3.5 m/s2 for at least half a second.

Long idle

Idling for more than 60 seconds on end.

High revs

Above 3,500 rpm for more than three seconds.

Speeding

Above your speed threshold for more than ten seconds.

Your car, not a textbook

Finding your sweet spot

From your real trips, TapeDash collects the steady stretches: acceleration under 0.3 m/s2 and throttle changing less than 5% a second. It bins them by 10 km/h of speed and reports kilometres a litre for a band once there are at least 3 km of data in it. The curve on the Coach page is yours, and the sweet spot is its peak.

Tips are facts, and never shame

They are written from templates, and each names a rupee amount and a cause: "₹24 of this trip went on idling, about 14 minutes with the engine on." Idling is called avoidable. Nothing is ever called wasted, and the comparison of a gentle drive with a hard one is a premise the simulator checks, not a claim made about any one trip.

Said plainly

What has been seen

Seen

  • On the simulator, the same route driven gently costs at least 15% less than driven hard, the hard run gets an idling or hard-acceleration tip and the gentle run gets none.
  • Tips never appear while the vehicle is moving, in widget tests at three screen sizes.
  • Event detection at each threshold's edges and durations.

Not yet

  • Whether the advice reads right on real drives. Review showed no tip on the first short real trip, which may well be correct.
  • A sweet spot from real data. It needs enough steady driving, and the real recordings so far are short.

TapeDash is a free, open-source, strictly offline Android app. Fuel and money figures are estimates. It is an aid, not a certified instrument: set it up while parked, and you remain responsible for driving safely.
