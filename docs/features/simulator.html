Features / How it is built

Built on a simulator

Everything is built and tested against a simulated car first. It has a mass, drag and a CVT, a warm-up, a fuel tank and exact ground truth, so the tests can ask whether the app's estimates are right, not just whether it runs.

  • Runs the whole test suite
  • Served to the real WiFi code over TCP
  • Profiles are approximations

Simulator first

Every feature is built against a car that is not there

The simulator is a feature of the project, not a test fixture. It powers every test, feeds the Design Lab, and can serve the real WiFi code on a laptop. Real adapters and a real car are connected only after every simulator test passes. It is not in the app: the app never shows simulated data as if it were your car.

The car

A simple physical model, with exact ground truth

Dynamics

About 1,150 kg with rolling resistance, aerodynamic drag, engine force from throttle, and braking to about 8 m/s2. Speed is never negative.

A CVT hatch

Idle about 750 rpm, revs that follow the throttle more than the speed, a 6,500 redline. A manual sporty profile has gears and a 7,000 redline.

Air and fuel that agree

Manifold pressure, air mass flow and fuel rate are physically consistent with each other, with fuel cut-off when coasting.

Warm-up, volts, fuel level

Coolant climbing from ambient to about 90 degrees over 8 to 10 minutes, 12.4 V off and about 14.1 V running, and a tank that empties.

The model keeps the exact cumulative fuel, distance, idle time and idle fuel, and a log of every scripted hard acceleration and hard braking. The tests compare the app's estimates with that. That is what lets them ask whether the app is right, not just whether it runs.

Many cars

Six profiles, and ten scripted drivers

Vehicle profiles

  • Petrol CVT with air flow (MAF).
  • Petrol CVT with manifold pressure only, so the app must use speed-density.
  • Petrol CVT that reports its own fuel rate.
  • Petrol manual, sporty, with gear ratios.
  • A minimal old car with only five readings, to test graceful degradation.
  • A diesel manual, whose money must be flagged low-confidence.

Scripted drivers

  • Cold start idle, city stop-go, highway cruise, a traffic jam.
  • Aggressive city, and the same route driven gently, so the coach's premise can be checked.
  • A mixed commute with the ignition off and on midway.
  • Repeated high-rev pulls on the sporty car.
  • A drive with two fault codes stored.
  • A flaky adapter with dropped replies and a reconnect.

The profiles are approximations and not real vehicle data. Real recordings from a real car tune them.

The adapter

An ELM327 that prints byte-accurate text, and misbehaves on request

It takes raw command strings and answers like a real adapter, with the reset banner, echo, spaces, linefeeds and the prompt following the current settings, the searching message on the first request, single and multi-PID requests, multi-frame replies split over lines, the supported-PID bitmasks, and fault codes. It can also be a bad adapter:

  • Latency of a Bluetooth Classic clone, for example 80 ms plus or minus 40.
  • Dropping the first command, ignoring the timeout command, refusing multi-PID requests.
  • Random NO DATA, a missing prompt, garbage bytes, and a disconnect at a chosen time.

Time is always injected, so a 30-minute drive runs in a few seconds on a fake clock, and the tests are deterministic.

Try it

Serve it over TCP and point the app at it

The same simulator is served over TCP by a tool, so the real WiFi code path can be tested end to end with no hardware. From an Android emulator the laptop is 10.0.2.2, a private address that passes the app's guard. Replayable recordings of every scenario can also be made, and real-car recordings become regression tests.

dart run tool/sim_server.dart --list
dart run tool/sim_server.dart \
  --scenario city_stop_go --profile petrol_cvt_map
dart run tool/record_sim.dart --all

Layers of tests

From a PID formula to a thirty-minute drive

Units

Every PID decoder at its edges, ELM framing in every setting, fault-code decoding, fuel estimators against hand-computed values, calibration, attribution and event detectors.

Simulator self-tests

Deterministic for a seed, consistent between air, pressure and fuel, and matching golden text for each adapter setting.

Simulated drives

The full stack on a fake clock: transport, ELM layer, poller, fuel, money, coach and trips, checked against ground truth.

Widgets and design

Every page against a simulated car at three sizes, plus golden images and a design lint, on CI.

Said plainly

What a simulator cannot tell you

What it proves

  • That the app's maths, state and screens are consistent with a car whose truth is known.
  • That the production WiFi path, parser and poller work against an adapter with awkward habits.

What it cannot

  • How a real car or a cheap clone adapter behaves. The real drives found things the simulator never would: two engine computers answering at once, and a 1.3 Hz poll rate.
  • That the profiles are right. They are approximations until real recordings tune them.

TapeDash is a free, open-source, strictly offline Android app. Fuel and money figures are estimates. It is an aid, not a certified instrument: set it up while parked, and you remain responsible for driving safely.
